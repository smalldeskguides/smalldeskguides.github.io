End-of-day routine

The five-minute desk reset

A short routine you can repeat every day is more useful than an occasional big clean-up. This one takes five minutes and leaves the desk ready for tomorrow.

The routine

  1. 1 minClear cups and scraps. Take dishes to the kitchen. Throw away wrappers, scrap paper and sticky notes you’re done with.
  2. 2 minReturn loose items to their spots. Put pens, chargers and notebooks back where they live, then empty your reset spot.
  3. 1 minWrite tomorrow’s first task. Put one concrete next action on a note in your work zone, like “Draft the intro for the client email” rather than “Work on project.”
  4. 1 minClear your work zone. Leave only your main device and tomorrow’s note in front of your chair. Wipe the surface if it needs it.

Set a phone timer for five minutes. When it goes off, stop, even if something is unfinished. The point is a routine small enough to repeat, not a perfect desk.

Why a timer helps

A short, fixed time limit makes the routine easy to start and keeps it from growing into a reorganizing project. If you keep finishing early, enjoy the extra minutes. If you keep running out of time, the desk probably holds more than its homes can fit; see give a small desk three homes.

Variations

  • Three-minute version: one minute each to clear, return and write tomorrow’s note.
  • Ten-minute weekly version: add sorting the paper pile, emptying your bag and plugging in anything that needs charging.
  • Shared desk: finish by putting your tray or bin away so the surface is clear for the next person.

Make it stick

  • Tie it to something you already do, like right before you close your laptop for the day.
  • Keep the checklist where you can see it. This page prints without the menu.
  • Don’t make up for missed days. The next reset still takes five minutes.
