Measure first

Before you buy a monitor stand, check three things

A monitor stand only helps if it puts the screen in the right place for you. These three checks take a few minutes and only need what you already have.

1. Screen height

Sit the way you normally work and look straight ahead. U.S. workplace-safety guidance says the top of the monitor should be at or slightly below eye level. If the top of your screen sits well below that, a stand may help. If it’s already at eye level, a stand won’t.

To find the height you need, stack books under the screen until the top lines up, then measure the stack. That number is the stand height to look for.

Bifocals or progressive lenses? OSHA suggests placing the monitor lower than usual, and possibly tilting it up, so you don’t tip your head back to read.

2. Screen distance

Put the screen directly in front of you, at least 20 inches (51 cm) away. OSHA gives 20 to 40 inches (50 to 100 cm) as the preferred range. An arm’s length is a quick way to check. Then measure how deep your desk is, because the stand, the screen and your keyboard all have to fit on it at that distance.

3. Keyboard height

Your keyboard should sit about at elbow height, with your elbows close to your body and your forearms roughly parallel to the floor. A monitor stand doesn’t change this. If the desk is too high for your arms, OSHA suggests raising the chair and adding a footrest instead.

If you’re raising a laptop, its keyboard goes up with the screen, so you’ll usually need a separate keyboard as well.

Your checklist

  • Stand height needed: the height of your book stack
  • Desk depth available: front edge to wall
  • Keyboard at about elbow height: yes or no

Sources: OSHA computer workstation guidance on monitors, keyboards and desks.
