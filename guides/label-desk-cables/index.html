Desk cables

Label both ends of every desk cable

When every plug looks the same, unplugging the right one means tracing it back by hand. Labeling both ends once saves you that hunt every time after.

What you need

You don’t need special labels to start. Any of these works:

  • Masking or painter’s tape and a marker
  • Paper scraps and clear tape
  • Plastic bread-bag clips you can write on

Step by step

  1. Trace one cable at a time. Follow it from the device to the outlet or power strip before you label anything. If you need to unplug or re-route a power cable, turn the device off first.
  2. Tag both ends with the same letter. Wrap tape all the way around the cable a couple of inches from each plug and write the same letter on both tags: A and A, B and B.
  3. Write down the key. Keep a short list, such as A for the monitor, B for the laptop and C for the lamp, on a card under the desk or in your phone’s notes.
  4. Leave slack where things move. A laptop you pick up, a monitor you adjust or a chair that rolls needs some loose cable, so plugs aren’t yanked or bent.
  5. Bundle loosely. Gather extra length with a twist tie or reusable strap you already have, without tight bends.

Safety basics: don’t run extension cords under carpets or furniture, don’t overload a cord beyond its capacity, and replace damaged cords instead of taping them up. Source: U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission.

Common mistakes

  • Labeling only one end. The plug at the power strip is the end you most need to identify.
  • Tags that slide off. Wrap the tape all the way around so it sticks to itself.
  • Pulling cables tight. Tight runs strain plugs and make it harder to move things.
